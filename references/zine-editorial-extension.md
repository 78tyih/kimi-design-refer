# Zine Editorial Extension

This extension adapts high-level visual mechanics from the MIT-licensed `LiamGvchi/gc-minimal-zine-poster` into Kimi Design Refer.

It does not replace the seven Kimi Core Families. It is an optional editorial/material layer or a direct `zine/*` family for poster, cover, social, editorial, and selected PPT/PDF work.

## Source system

The source project defines one parent visual system:

`poetic paper-texture + 70–90% negative space + one small visual event + sparse editorial type + one strong chromatic accent + scanned/printed reproduction`

Its variation engine defines 10 layout families:

1. `center-fragment`
2. `lower-left-float`
3. `upper-right-block`
4. `dual-panel`
5. `irregular-cutout`
6. `type-led`
7. `dot-orbit`
8. `single-specimen`
9. `diagonal-notes`
10. `edge-counterweight`

These are layout grammars rather than ten fully independent brands. Kimi Design Refer distills them into five stable visual families.

## 1. zine/micro-archive — 微型档案

Combines `center-fragment`, `lower-left-float`, `single-specimen`.

75–90% open paper, one tiny image/object/color block, archive microtext, one note-like sentence, scan grain. Best for one idea, one quote, article covers, research chapter openers. Focal event roughly 8–20% of the frame. Use typewriter, old serif, mono, or restrained tiny sans. One strong accent is carried by the specimen/block.

Pairs best with `gray-proofboard`, `cosmic-index`, `white-lab`.

## 2. zine/dual-memory — 双联记忆

Combines `dual-panel` and paired-state logic.

Two small adjacent or slightly overlapping fragments with a narrow gap; their relation carries meaning. Best for before/after, compare/contrast, source/result, signal/noise, two market states. Together the panels usually occupy 12–28% of the frame. One panel may carry the accent while the other remains grayscale.

Pairs best with `kinetic-glyph`, `gray-proofboard`, `cosmic-index`.

## 3. zine/color-cutout — 色块剪贴

Combines `upper-right-block`, `irregular-cutout`.

One torn, organic, geometric, or opaque chromatic shape interacts with a photo/print fragment. The cutout is a compositional actor, not decoration. Best for tension, interruption, uncertainty and bold campaign covers. Use one high-chroma hue such as cobalt, ultramarine, red, yellow, cyan or violet.

Pairs best with `gray-proofboard`, `simulation-window`, `cosmic-index`.

Avoid scrapbook clutter, tape/stickers everywhere, many torn pieces and multicolor mood boards.

## 4. zine/type-relic — 文字遗物

Combines `type-led`.

Typography becomes the visual object. One word, phrase, numeral, or character is treated as a printed specimen with rough letterpress/xerox/registration behavior. Best for slogans, versions, chapter openers, conceptual statements and one-word campaigns.

Pairs best with `kinetic-glyph`, `cosmic-index`.

Avoid quote cards, competing typefaces, long paragraphs and random multilingual collage.

## 5. zine/orbit-notes — 漂移注记

Combines `dot-orbit`, `diagonal-notes`, `edge-counterweight`.

One small subject is balanced by distant microtext, dots, fine lines, dates, coordinates, or notes. Visual energy comes from spacing and directional drift. Best for research notes, maps, location/time metaphors, system states and abstract relations.

Pairs best with `black-orbit`, `cosmic-index`, `pixel-moon`.

Avoid decorative constellations with no semantic role, generic node diagrams and filling empty space with labels.

## Shared Zine DNA

When `zine/*` is active:
- default to vertical editorial ratios such as 3:5, 4:5, or user-specified;
- keep 70–90% open field;
- use one primary visual event, not a full scene;
- use paper, xerox, risograph, halftone, scan noise, letterpress bleed, or slight misregistration;
- use one visible high-chroma accent;
- keep text sparse and note-like;
- keep reproduction flat, low-to-medium contrast, and non-glossy.

## Kimi × Zine equation

`quiet field + one small evidence event + technical/editorial hierarchy + one chromatic anchor + local print imperfection`

Kimi contributes procedural logic, evidence framing, precise spacing, system thinking and technical annotation.

Zine contributes paper/print tactility, poetic reduction, asymmetric editorial drift, one saturated emotional color and intimate archival texture.

## Hybrid intensity

- `zine-light` — 15–25%: texture + one zine typography/accent behavior; Kimi remains dominant.
- `zine-medium` — 30–45%: one distilled zine family while keeping Kimi hierarchy and system logic.
- `zine-full` — 60–80%: primarily zine editorial, with Kimi supplying precision/evidence logic.

For websites and dense infographics default to `zine-light`. For posters/covers, `zine-medium` or `zine-full` is allowed. For PPT/PDF use stronger zine treatment mainly on title, divider, chapter and editorial pages.

Do not indiscriminately add beige paper, torn edges or typewriter text to every Kimi family. Paper texture alone does not count as zine editorial.
