# Kimi Design Refer

This is an aesthetic reference and routing layer, not an official Kimi brand kit.

## Design equation

`quiet field + bounded evidence system + technical hierarchy + visible process + restrained anomaly`

## 1. Choose the medium

Use one primary medium: `web-ui`, `ppt-pdf`, `poster-social`, `infographic`, `motion`, or `review-only`.

Medium behavior:
- **web-ui:** preserve usability and real HTML/CSS text; prefer large fields, numbered sections, evidence windows, restrained interaction.
- **ppt-pdf:** use a visual thesis across title, chapter, evidence, data and closing pages; keep exact copy readable.
- **poster-social:** reduce the brief to one imageable proposition and one focal event.
- **infographic:** accuracy first; use semantic layout and editable/vector structure when possible.
- **motion:** choose one motion verb such as orbit, converge, calibrate, reveal, index, dissolve or simulate.
- **review-only:** score family fidelity, negative space, hierarchy, process, color discipline, originality and readability.

## 3. Composition rules

Always:
- keep one quiet field;
- keep one dominant evidence or visual-event cluster;
- use negative space as structure;
- show at least one reasoning trace: orbit, measurement, index, point cloud, repeated state, transformation, trajectory, annotation, or simulation;
- establish hierarchy through scale, spacing and isolation rather than bold text everywhere;
- keep decorative detail subordinate.

## 4. Typography and color

Kimi Core defaults:
- black `#000000`
- charcoal `#17191A`
- soft white `#F5F5F5`
- mid gray `#7A7A7A`
- proof gray `#C1C1C1`
- cool lab `#DCE3DE`

Use modern sans, editorial serif, mono, pixel type, or glyph display according to the selected family. Technical microtype is for indices, dates, measurements, coordinates and state labels.

For Core Kimi work, accent color is punctuation rather than atmosphere. Avoid generic blue-purple SaaS gradients.

For `zine/*`, follow the extension rule: one visible high-chroma anchor on a restrained paper/grayscale field.

## 5. Kimi × Zine blending

Use one Kimi Core Family plus at most one Zine Family.

Intensity:
- `zine-light` 15–25% — good for web UI and dense information.
- `zine-medium` 30–45% — good for covers, chapter pages and campaign posters.
- `zine-full` 60–80% — primarily zine editorial, with Kimi precision/evidence logic remaining visible.

Do not add beige paper, torn edges or typewriter text unless the zine system is actually active.

## 6. Quality gate

A strong result should pass these checks:
1. one clear primary family;
2. one clear focal/evidence system;
3. intentional negative space;
4. clear display-vs-annotation hierarchy;
5. visible process/reasoning trace;
6. disciplined color;
7. no generic SaaS/cyberpunk fallback;
8. no random scrapbook clutter;
9. accurate/readable content;
10. original composition rather than a copied campaign frame.

When `zine/*` is active, also require 70–90% open field, one primary visual event, one chromatic anchor, coherent print/scan texture and restrained editorial typography.

## 7. Originality boundary

Never use Kimi/Moonshot logos unless the user supplies authorized assets and explicitly asks for them. Do not reproduce a known Kimi campaign frame, proprietary illustration, exact lockup, or source zine example. Learn the higher-level grammar and translate the user's own subject into it.
